Made For Color Blind Eyes

You already know the problem: the swatch doesn't help. ColorSeeker doesn't ask you to judge a shade — it just tells you. Point the camera and read the answer in plain text: "Crimson", #DC143C, RGB 220/20/60. Red-green color blindness makes maroon, crimson and brown a coin flip. Sorting laundry, picking ripe fruit, reading a resistor, telling a Cat6 jacket from a fiber one — the app reports a name and a number every time, so you don't have to guess and you don't have to ask anyone. Colour blind? Same app — we just spell it the other way.

🔬 Why It Works When Other Apps Don't

Most color apps compare raw RGB, so a shadow reads as a different color and you get "Brown" for a mustard shirt in a dim room. ColorSeeker converts what it sees into CIE L*a*b* — the color space built to match human vision — and when it names a color it deliberately discounts brightness, weighting the true color axes instead. Dim light, harsh light, shadow: same answer.

✨ Key Features

Read It, Don't Guess It

Point at any object and get its name (e.g., "Mustard", "Coral", "Crimson") plus exact Hex/RGB codes instantly, from a 58-color palette of real names. No trusting the swatch, no second opinion.

Show Me What I Can't See

Red berries in a green tree. A red wire in a bundle. A pink flag in tall grass. Tap to lock a color and ColorSeeker highlights every match in frame and counts them — the things your eyes blend into the background, pulled out and marked.

Smart Object Counting

Automatically highlight and count matching objects in real-time. Perfect for inventory, hobbies, or sorting mixed parts. Count hundreds of items in seconds just by spreading them out.

Precision Zoom

Switch between 0.6x wide, 1x standard and 3x close to read small objects like beads, screws, or resistors from across the bench. Shadow-proof naming sees through dim lighting.

Fiber Aqua, Cat6 Blue, Safety Orange and Earth Green sit in the palette next to the designer colors — because telling one cable jacket from another in a dim closet shouldn't take a second opinion.
