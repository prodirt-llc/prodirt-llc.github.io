How to Calculate Even Spacing (The Formula)

If you want to do it by hand, here's the whole thing. To space n pieces evenly across a length L, where each piece is w wide and a piece sits at each end of the run:

On-center spacing = (L − w) ÷ (n − 1)

That gives you the distance from the center of one piece to the center of the next. The clear gap between pieces is simply:

Gap = on-center − w

The reason you measure on-center rather than gap-to-gap: mark every center from one end with a tape, and a rounding error can't accumulate. Measure each gap in turn and the errors stack — by the seventh picket you're visibly out.

Worked example — imperial

A deck rail with 96" between posts, 8 balusters, each 1.5" wide:

  • On-center = (96 − 1.5) ÷ (8 − 1) = 94.5 ÷ 7 = 13.5"
  • Gap between balusters = 13.5 − 1.5 = 12"
  • Check: 8 balusters × 1.5" = 12", plus 7 gaps × 12" = 84". 12 + 84 = 96" ✓

Worked example — metric

A fence panel 2000 mm wide, 6 pickets, each 50 mm wide:

  • On-center = (2000 − 50) ÷ (6 − 1) = 1950 ÷ 5 = 390 mm
  • Gap between pickets = 390 − 50 = 340 mm
  • Check: 6 pickets × 50 mm = 300 mm, plus 5 gaps × 340 mm = 1700 mm. 300 + 1700 = 2000 mm ✓

Real numbers rarely land that clean. Divide 2400 mm by 7 and you get 342.857… — and that's the point where the scrap paper starts. The app does this division for you, rounds every position to something you can actually mark (a whole millimetre, or a fraction down to 1/64"), and lists every center from one end so nothing drifts. It also handles edge offsets when the run doesn't start flush, and shows the layout as a diagram before you cut.
